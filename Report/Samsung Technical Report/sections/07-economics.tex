\section{Cost}
\label{sec:economics}

Every figure in this section is read from a provider's billing record or from
the team's own account records. Nothing here is projected.

The split between those two lines is the more useful fact. Running the finished
system is cheap. Building it was not, because development re-ran the expensive
half repeatedly: every change to the ingestion pipeline meant re-reading the
specification from scratch, and the player and investigator ran on a more
expensive tier until the quality cost of moving them down had been measured.

No other line in this project cost money. There was no accelerator hardware, no
server, and no licence fee: the graph database, the service frameworks, the
browser and device drivers, the embedding model and the document-handling
libraries are all open-source and self-hosted, the development machines and the
test handset were already owned, and an Android emulator on each machine
carried the great majority of execution.

    \toprule
    Unit & Measured basis & Cost \\

    One Android test executed & 10.1 device steps across 232 stored observations; accessibility tree averaging 4{,}686 bytes per step; planner logging 384{,}000 tokens over 108 calls & USD 0.008 \\
    \addlinespace
    One specification ingestion & About 22 calls on the most expensive tier, three self-consistency samples, no output cap & USD 1.80 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Within the per-test figure the player accounts for about 92\,\% and the planner
about 8\,\%. That is why reducing median steps from 50 to 9 was a real saving
and why optimising the planner prompt would not have been.

Ingestion is paid once per document rather than once per run, so it is a fixed
cost. It is also the largest single line item in the project's own spending,
for the reason given above: it was paid again every time the ingestion pipeline
itself changed. Three metering controls would reduce it and are not yet built:
an output ceiling on extraction, a reduced sample count once extraction has
stabilised, and a cache that refuses to re-read an unchanged document.

\subsection{The shape of it}

A campaign costs less than a dollar. A specification ingestion costs about two
hundred test executions. The entire project, including every development run
and every re-ingestion, cost under fifty dollars of inference and nothing else.

For an organisation running this against a build, the implication is that
inference is not the constraint on how much testing gets done. A five-hundred
test suite is about four dollars of inference. The constraint is wall clock,
which is emulator time rather than model time, and the engineer time spent
confirming candidate defects.
